## Problem Statement

Penny answers spending questions over one 300-row CSV that was enriched once, offline. The founder wants a product: a company or bank connects its own financial data, in whatever form it has, the data stays in sync continuously, every stage cross-verifies itself without a human, and a chatbot answers questions about spending, income, and profit as of the latest committed data. The founder wants this to be a profitable tech startup and eventually to sell to banks, companies over their own books, and fintech vendors.

Founder's words: "make some reusable connector or container where user will drop their data with any format and then this project will sync on the fly and realtime and started acting as a chatbot with latest data. make sure all the pipeline should crossverify without manual intervention."

## Demand Evidence

None yet. This is a thesis. When asked for a named buyer, the founder answered "make sure it should sell to any of these 4 ... consider all above options 1,2,3,4," which is a platform vision, not a customer. The independent cold read named the same tell. The only reachable person is "a small company I know personally." Demand evidence is the first milestone's job, not an input to this design.

## Status Quo

Presumed, not observed: the small company runs a spreadsheet plus a bookkeeper, and possibly QuickBooks or Xero. The bookkeeper reconciles bank lines against the books by hand each month. Nobody opens the accounting dashboard to ask a question. This must be observed in the first week (see The Assignment).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- Target: the owner or ops lead at one small company the founder can reach this week, and their bookkeeper.
- Wedge (Milestone 1): they upload three months of real bank and card exports, ask their three real monthly questions, and the bookkeeper compares the chat's totals against their own books by hand and agrees they match. "Numbers you can show your accountant" is the product. The chatbot is the interface to it. Bookkeeping data enters in Milestone 2.
- Banks and fintech vendors are later channels, earned after this segment works.

## Constraints

- Solo founder, pre-product, no revenue.
- Penny's invariants hold: the LLM never does arithmetic, tools are the source of every number, the transaction path is read-only, dependencies point inward and `tests/architecture/test_dependency_rule.py` enforces it.
- Accounting OAuth apps (Intuit, Xero) and any direct bank API onboarding take days to weeks to approve. They must not gate the first customer test.
- Financial data means per-tenant isolation, encryption at rest, and an audit trail from the first milestone that touches real customer data, not later. Local development uses fixtures only; the customer's real exports are handled only in the deployed environment.
- One reporting currency per tenant through Milestone 3.
- Deployment home is the founder's own AWS account, one region chosen by where the first customer's data may live. The model is Claude via Bedrock through a VPC interface endpoint with Bedrock Guardrails; no public model API; the self-hosted `local` provider is the documented exit (revision 2).

## Premises

1. First customer is one small company the founder knows. Tenant id on every table from day one; the founder provisions the tenant by hand; no tenant admin or signup UI until a second tenant exists.
2. "Any format" is three ingress kinds, not N parsers: direct bank APIs where a bank offers one to its customer, accounting APIs (QuickBooks, Xero), and file uploads (CSV, XLSX, OFX/QFX, PDF statements). For a new source signature the AI proposes a column mapping once, it is cached like `data/merchant_catalog.json`, and every later row is applied deterministically.
3. Revised after the cold read. Cross-verification is two-source matching, not an AI judge: every cash-touching book entry matches a bank posting and every bank posting matches a cash-touching book entry, with the unmatched set surfaced as the sync report. The balance identity (opening plus postings equals closing) runs when a source carries balances. Row invariants and duplicate detection run always. AI labels (merchant, category) carry confidence and get sampled judge review, but a label can never change a total or block a sync.
4. Real-time is event-driven incremental sync: webhooks where a source offers them, polling otherwise, into an append-only ledger with supersession and tenant-global sync versions. The chatbot answers against one consistent snapshot version per request. The freshness target is measured from webhook receipt or scheduled poll to committed snapshot; upstream bank or accounting-provider latency is outside the system and excluded.
5. Penny's read-only tool architecture stays. Profit needs the accounting side, so the domain grows from one `Transaction` type to accounts, entities, and both sides of the ledger.
6. Hosted SaaS. Founder-provisioned tenants through Milestone 3; self-serve signup is revisited when a second tenant exists. Banks and fintech vendors become channels only after one company segment works.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: File-drop first, connector port shaped for later
Extend Penny in place with an upload path, AI-proposed cached mapping, reconciliation on commit, a Postgres ledger with sync versions, and a source-adapter port shaped for later live connectors. Effort M (human ~3 weeks, CC+gstack ~2-3 days). Completeness 6/10. Advisor's recommendation. Not chosen: founder chose the full platform; A survives as Milestone 1 of B.

### Approach B: Connector core plus ledger service (chosen)
A separate ingestion service with one source-adapter port and three adapter kinds, a mapping catalog, a two-source reconciliation engine, an append-only multi-tenant ledger in Postgres, webhooks plus a worker, and Penny reading a new repository port over accounts and both ledger sides. Effort XL (human ~3 months, CC+gstack ~2-3 weeks). Completeness 10/10.

### Approach C: Accounting system as the source of truth
Sync only from QuickBooks or Xero, treat their reconciled ledger as canonical, file upload as fallback. Effort S-M. Completeness 5/10. Not chosen: excludes any company without accounting software, and needs an Intuit or Xero developer app before the first customer test.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B, by founder decision, sequenced so that Milestone 1 is Approach A and the demand interview happens in week one, Milestone 1 is the product demand test, and provider approvals proceed in parallel.

### Package ownership and dependency rule

- `penny/domain/` stays stdlib-only and is shared by all three packages.
- `penny_ledger/` owns the ledger schema, Alembic migrations, and both the write repository (used by ingestion) and the read repository (used by chat). It depends only on `penny.domain` and the database driver.
- `penny_ingest/` owns adapters, the mapping catalog, enrichment, reconciliation, and the job worker. It imports `penny.domain` and `penny_ledger`. It must not import `penny.application`, `penny.infrastructure`, or `penny.presentation`.
- `penny/` (chat) imports `penny.domain` and `penny_ledger`. It must not import `penny_ingest`.
- `tests/architecture/test_dependency_rule.py` gains these three rules.

### Domain model (extends `penny/domain/`)

- `Tenant(id, name, contact_name, contact_email, reporting_currency)` scopes everything. The founder sets all five fields when provisioning. Every table has `tenant_id`; every repository method takes a tenant scope.
- `Source(id, tenant_id, kind, credentials_ref, status, coverage_through)`. Kinds: `direct_bank`, `accounting`, `file`. A file source is one per (tenant, account); its signature lives on the confirmed mapping, not on the source, so a bank changing its export columns produces a new mapping, never a second source. `coverage_through` is the latest posting date the source has delivered.
- `Account(id, tenant_id, name, type, currency, side)`. Types: bank, card, receivable, payable, revenue, expense. Side is `bank` or `book`. An account is a physical account and can be fed by more than one source through `AccountSource(account_id, source_id, external_id, attached_version)`, which is what lets a customer start with files and attach a live feed later. An account whose currency differs from the tenant's reporting currency is flagged at discovery and excluded from totals and matching through Milestone 3; conversion is deferred.
- `AccountLink(id, tenant_id, bank_account_id, book_account_id, confirmed_by, created_version, retired_version)` pairs a bank-side account with the book-side account that records it. Links are proposed by name and balance similarity and confirmed in the connect UI (Milestone 2). Postings on a bank account with no link are reported as "no book account linked", never as unmatched.
- `Posting(id, tenant_id, account_id, day, amount, direction, description, merchant, category, confidence, row_hash, native_id, status, supersedes_id, created_version, retired_version, provenance)`. The existing `Transaction` is a projection of active bank-side and card-side postings in the reporting currency, so the ten existing insights keep working. The projection derives the category at read time: a posting that is a member of an active `TransferPair` at the snapshot version is presented as `Transfers`, the taxonomy's existing non-spend category in `penny/domain/taxonomy.py`, and the stored category is untouched. Any other label change, including an accepted relabel proposal, inserts a superseding row at a new sync version and retires the old one, so a `Figure` computed earlier still reproduces exactly.
- `BookEntry(id, tenant_id, account_id, day, amount, counterparty, doc_type, doc_ref, native_id, status, supersedes_id, created_version, retired_version)`. Doc types: `payment`, `deposit`, `expense_claim`, `transfer`, `bank_journal` (cash-touching, participate in matching); `invoice`, `bill`, `credit_note`, `journal` (not cash-touching, excluded from bank matching).
- `Match(id, tenant_id, posting_id, book_entry_id, method, score, created_version, retired_version)`. Methods through Milestone 3: `exact`, `tolerance`, `split`. `manual` arrives in Milestone 4 with the bookkeeper action described under Connect flow.
- `TransferPair(id, tenant_id, posting_a, posting_b, created_version, retired_version)` for internal transfers.
- `SyncRun(id, tenant_id, source_id, sync_version, started, finished, rows_in, rows_rejected, rows_skipped, rows_deduped, rows_retired, balance_check, transfer_pairs_formed, unmatched_postings, unmatched_entries, warnings, status)`.
- `Figure(id, tenant_id, conversation_id, snapshot_version, computation_key, parameters, value)`, persisted for the conversation's lifetime, so a number can be explained later.

Row invariants live on the entities as they do today: amount is finite, direction agrees with sign, currency matches the account, and the day parses and falls inside the source's declared range. The declared range is the statement period for OFX/QFX and PDF, the cursor window for APIs, and for CSV/XLSX the invariant reduces to "the date parses under the confirmed format," since those files declare no period. A zero-amount row (an informational interest line, a reversed fee) is skipped and counted in `rows_skipped`, never rejected, because it cannot affect a balance identity. Rejection is reserved for rows whose amount or date cannot be determined.

### Supersession and versioning

- `sync_version` is a tenant-global monotonic sequence assigned at commit. Every commit, from any source, takes the next number.
- A row is active at version V when `created_version <= V` and `retired_version` is null or greater than V. `snapshot(V)` is exactly that set. The chat service resolves the latest committed V at the start of a request and pins every tool call to it.
- A source that reports a modification (a direct bank API's modified record, an edited or voided invoice) inserts a new row with `supersedes_id` pointing at the old one and retires the old row at the same commit. A removal or pending-to-posted transition retires the old row and inserts the replacement if any. Nothing is ever updated in place.
- File re-upload semantics: a file has no native ids and cannot emit modify or remove, so every upload declares a coverage window, the minimum to maximum parsed date. When such retirements would occur, the run first pauses in needs_confirmation with a preview of the counts (design D21); after "Replace", every previously active row from the same file source inside that window that is absent from the new file is retired, counted in `rows_retired`, and listed in the report. Rows outside the window are untouched. This is how a corrected export replaces a wrong one through Milestone 3.
- Feed cutover (Milestone 3): when a live feed is attached to an account already fed by files, the feed's first commit retires every file-source posting inside the feed's backfill window, counted in `rows_retired` and listed in the report, so the overlap is never active twice. File postings before the backfill window stay active.
- When either side of a `Match` or `TransferPair` is retired, the match is retired at the same commit and both surviving items return to the unmatched pool for re-matching.

### Ingress kinds and adapters

One `SourceAdapter` protocol in `penny_ingest/ports.py`:

- `discover(credentials) -> list[Account]` (for a file source, returns the account proposed by the mapping pass and confirmed by the user, or the existing account whose confirmed mapping matched, design D15)
- `pull(account, since_cursor) -> (list[RawRecord], next_cursor)` where each `RawRecord` carries an operation of `add`, `modify`, or `remove` and the source's native id when one exists
- `subscribe(callback) -> bool` (False when the source has no webhooks; the worker polls instead)

Adapters, in delivery order:

1. `FileUploadAdapter`. An upload endpoint only; no watched folder. The file is uploaded first; the mapping pass proposes the account (name, type, currency) and the user confirms or changes it (design D15); one account per file, and multi-account files are out of scope. Same-source dedup is therefore scoped by (tenant, account, kind=file). Detects CSV and XLSX in Milestone 1, OFX/QFX and PDF in Milestone 4. For CSV and XLSX the normalized header row plus stable type classes per column forms the source signature. OFX/QFX are parsed with a standard library, no AI, and carry FITID as the native id. PDF statements are extracted to tables first, then treated as a CSV signature. In Milestone 2 the same adapter gains a book-side mapping (date, amount, counterparty, doc type, doc ref, account) so a company without an accounting API can upload a ledger export.
2. `AccountingAdapter`. The port is shaped for both QuickBooks Online and Xero, and only the provider the first company uses is implemented in Milestone 2; the other waits for a customer. OAuth app per provider. Pulls accounts, invoices, bills, payments, journal entries, each with its native id, and reports edits and voids as `modify` and `remove`. Both providers offer webhooks; backfill is paginated polling.
3. `DirectBankAdapter`, one per bank, only where the customer's bank offers an API to the customer directly (R7, D9). No third-party aggregator: Plaid and Flinks are removed from the design under the enclave rule. Where no direct API exists, the source stays file-based with scheduled upload reminders.

Bank data via a direct bank API and bank data via a file upload land on the same physical `Account` through `AccountSource`, so a customer can start with files and attach a live feed later; the cutover rule under Supersession retires the overlapping file rows at the feed's first commit.

### Mapping catalog

`data/mapping_catalog.json` in development, a `mappings` table in production. Keyed by the source signature: normalized headers plus stable type classes only, never value lengths or emptiness, so the same bank produces the same signature every month. On a new signature the ingestion service asks the model (Bedrock via the private endpoint) for a map covering: date column and date format, amount column or debit/credit column pair, sign convention, decimal separator and thousands separator, description, balance column if present, currency, native id column if present, the proposed account (name, type, currency), the ambiguity detail for any field that has one, and a confidence. The map is confirmed automatically at confidence 0.9 or above; below that, or whenever the date format is ambiguous in the sample (any value that parses under both day-first and month-first), or whenever the numeric convention is ambiguous (values that parse under both `1,234.56` and `1.234,56`), or whenever a required column is missing, the source enters `needs_confirmation` and the connect UI shows the mapping confirm screen in one of three shapes (design D16). Confirmed maps apply deterministically forever; the model is never called on a known signature. Same shape as the merchant catalog and for the same reason: the mapping is a property of the source, not of the row.

### Reconciliation engine (the trust story)

Runs on every sync, per tenant. Stages 1 through 5 run before commit, stage 6 is the commit, stage 7 runs after it.

1. Parse and invariants over the complete row set of the batch. A failed row is rejected and named in the report. On a balance-bearing source, one rejected row makes the balance identity unprovable, so the whole batch is blocked (stage 2). On a source without balances, the remaining rows proceed and the rejection is reported.
2. Balance identity, on file sources only through Milestone 3 (direct bank API and accounting sources expose point-in-time balances, not per-batch opening and closing figures, and are not balance-bearing). When the file provides opening and closing balances, opening plus the signed sum of every parsed row, including rows that will later be deduplicated, must equal closing to the cent. When it provides a running balance column instead, rows are sorted by date and then file order, opening is the first row's running balance minus its signed amount, closing is the last row's running balance, and every row's running balance is also checked against the cumulative sum so the first failing row can be named. A miss blocks the commit of that source's batch and puts the source in the Attention state.
3. Duplicate detection at commit time only. `row_hash` is the native id when the source provides one (FITID, the bank API's transaction id, QuickBooks or Xero id); otherwise it is (account, day, amount, normalized description, occurrence index within the file), so two identical purchases on the same day are two rows. Cross-version dedup suppresses only rows already committed from the same source, which is what makes overlapping re-uploads safe. Same-day same-amount repeats across different sources are a warning in the report, never a drop.
4. Internal transfer pairing, re-run over all unpaired active postings for the tenant on every commit, so a card export uploaded a day after the bank export still pairs. Candidates are opposite-sign postings of the same amount across two different accounts of type bank or card within 3 days. Pairing is one-to-one and exclusive; ties break deterministically by closest day, then by description hints such as "transfer" or "payment", then by file order. Every newly formed pair is listed in the report as a reviewable item. Paired postings are presented as `Transfers` at read time and excluded from income and expense totals.
5. Two-source matching whenever the tenant has both a bank-side and a book-side source, run over all unmatched active items for the tenant on every commit, so a book entry recorded a week after the bank posting still matches. Only cash-touching doc types participate. Passes in order, one-to-one and exclusive: exact (same amount, same account pairing, within 3 days); tolerance (within the larger of 5 cents or 0.5 percent, within 5 days, for FX and fee differences); split (up to 5 book entries summing to one posting within 7 days, or the reverse). Whatever remains is the unmatched set. Open invoices and bills are reported in their own AR/AP section, not as unmatched. Unmatched never blocks a commit; it is the product.
6. Commit is atomic per source batch: one new tenant-global `sync_version`, all rows or none.
7. Label drift, after commit and asynchronously, delivered in Milestone 4 (the worker's drift tick is inert until then): category and merchant distribution per account compared with the trailing window. Drift beyond threshold enqueues a sampled judge review using the existing `eval/` judge. The review produces a report item and, optionally, a relabel proposal with confidence; it never changes a label on its own and never alters a total. The bookkeeper accepts or dismisses the proposal on the source's report in the connect screen, which writes an audit record; an accepted proposal inserts superseding rows at a new version. The flag clears on accept or dismiss, or when the next batch's distribution is back within threshold.

Stages 1 through 6 are deterministic. Stage 7 is the only place a model participates in verification, and it cannot alter a number. That is what "cross-verify without manual intervention" means in this design.

### Real-time sync

- The ingestion image runs one worker loop over a Postgres job table (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED). Webhooks from Xero and Intuit, and from a direct bank API where offered, insert a pull job for the affected account. The same loop ticks every five minutes to poll sources without webhooks, backfill gaps, and run drift reviews. No external queue.
- Every successful pull commits a `SyncRun` and a new `sync_version`. Invariant rejections on API sources never block; they are reported per row. An API batch fails to commit only on an infrastructure error, in which case the cursor is not advanced, the job is retried with exponential backoff, and one alert is sent per distinct cause until it clears. A dead credential (401, 403, revocation) moves the source to needs_reauth and stops retries (R8, D10). A live feed therefore never enters Attention for data reasons through Milestone 3.
- Freshness metric: time from webhook receipt or scheduled poll to committed snapshot, target under 60 seconds. Upstream bank and accounting-provider latency is excluded and reported separately as source lag.

### Chat service changes (existing `penny/`)

- `TransactionRepository` is kept as a compatibility port. A new `LedgerRepository` port adds accounts, book entries, matches, and `snapshot(tenant_id, version)`, which returns an immutable `TransactionRepository` view (R2, D4). `Container.for_request(ctx)` builds `InsightService`, `PromptAssembler`, `GreetingFacts` and tool bindings per request from that view; `RequestContext` carries `tenant_id` and `snapshot_version` for attribution. Views are cached in a bounded LRU keyed by (tenant, version) (R12, D14). Amounts are `Decimal` in the domain and NUMERIC(18,2) in the ledger; the projection converts to float at this boundary only (R1, D3).
- Composition root swaps the JSON repository for the ledger one behind a setting, so the existing tests and the component gallery still run against the fixture.
- `InsightService` gains income, expense, profit, receivables, and payables over `BookEntry`, and cash-versus-accrual views where both sides exist.
- Two new tools, both Milestone 2: `get_profit_and_loss(period)` and `explain_number(figure_id)`. Every numeric component carries a `figure_id`; the `Figure` record stores the snapshot version, computation key, and parameters, and `explain_number` resolves against the recorded version, never the latest, so an explanation always reproduces the number the customer saw.
- Data context prompt states the snapshot version, each source's coverage date and last sync time, whether any source is blocked and since which version, and the unmatched count, so the model can say "12 bank lines are not yet in your books" or "your card feed has been blocked since Tuesday" instead of guessing. The "data as of" line is rendered by the client from GET /snapshot: the latest coverage_through plus the limiting or disconnected source (design D20); the greeting prompt does not restate the date.

### Connect flow (UI)

One screen added to the existing phone frame: connect your data. A divided list of accounts inside Penny's existing chrome (design D10). Adding a source opens a bottom sheet with three choices: upload a file, connect accounting, connect a bank. Each account row shows one of: verified with counts and coverage date; needs you (a mapping or account proposal to confirm, an account link to confirm in Milestone 2, a balance miss in the file); disconnected with a Reconnect action (R8, D10); or failed on our side. A pending run renders as a sub-row beneath its account (reading, checking totals, needs confirmation including the re-upload preview, blocked, failed) (design D8, D12, D15, D21). Uploads return 202 and the screen polls the run (R5, D7). Attention names the cause: a balance miss with the expected and actual closing figures, or the rejected rows with reasons. Through Milestone 3 the resolutions are "Export it again from your bank" and "Tell us about this file", which sends the run id and row numbers only (design D18); a corrected export replaces rows in its window only after the preview is confirmed (design D21); an audited override with a typed reason arrives in Milestone 4, alongside the bookkeeper's manual match and unmatch action, which records who, when, and why, and never alters a total. Alerts for Attention go to the tenant's contact email and to the founder. Tenants are provisioned by the founder; there is no signup screen. Wireframe: see Approved Mockups (wireframe-v2.png).

### Security and tenancy

From Milestone 1, because that is when real customer data arrives:

- Tenant id on every row and in every query; a repository test asserts no cross-tenant read is possible.
- Managed Postgres with encryption at rest; TLS everywhere.
- Audit trail records every upload, mapping confirmation, sync, override, and chat turn in the `audit_events` table (below).
- Credentials for OAuth tokens live in a secrets store, referenced by id, never in the ledger.
- Prompts carry only tool output, never raw source payloads; mapping and merchant resolution send digit-masked descriptors only.
- Enclave rule (user instruction, R4 D6, R7 D9; model posture revised in revision 2): no tenant data is shared with any other tenant or any third party. Inference runs on Claude via Bedrock inside the founder's own AWS account, reached only through a VPC interface endpoint, with Bedrock Guardrails on every prompt and response and no data retention; this is the one deliberate exception to the strictest reading, the same posture as the bank reference architecture, and the self-hosted `local` provider remains the exit. The ledger and ingest services have no network egress except the Bedrock endpoint and the customer's own accounting provider or bank; the merchant catalog is per tenant; per-tenant envelope encryption covers descriptor, counterparty and provenance columns with a tenant data key wrapped by a KMS key.

### Pass 7 decisions

1. Account before upload (D15 = 1A): file first; the upload is not keyed by an account or source, the account is attached on confirmation. The mapping pass also proposes the account: name from the institution in the header or filename, type bank or card from the sign pattern and column names, currency from the file. The run's first user-facing state is one line, "This looks like a chequing account at RBC in CAD", with Confirm and a Change link; Change exposes name, type and currency. A file whose confirmed mapping matches exactly one existing account skips the step and the line reads "Adding to RBC Chequing"; if it matches more than one account at the same bank, the user is asked which. This replaces the engineering plan's "pick or create the account first" card; the ledger model (one account per file, AccountSource) is unchanged.
2. Mapping confirm (D16 = 2A): three shapes of one screen. One ambiguous field: the headline is the question ("Is 03/08/2026 the 3rd of August?") with two tappable readings, each showing the same two sample rows re-read; the full mapping table is collapsed under "All columns". Several uncertain fields: the full table with every Penny-reads-it-as cell a picker. Required column missing (date or amount): the screen says so, shows the file's headers, and offers a column picker for that field or "This file won't work, try another export". One primary button in all three: "Looks right, import <n> rows". Replaces "one-click confirm" in the engineering plan's mapping section; the 0.9 auto-confirm threshold is unchanged.
3. Following a run (D17 = 3A): after the 202 the client polls GET /runs/{run_id} at 2s for 30s, then 5s, then 15s, stopping at a terminal state (committed, blocked, failed, needs_confirmation). The run id is kept in client state and in the URL on web, so returning to the screen resumes polling. iOS: background refresh in Milestone 1 and push in Milestone 2 mark the run done while the app is closed, polling on foreground as the fallback. Poll failure shows "Still working, connection lost" and keeps retrying. The connect list always reflects server state on load; nothing lives only on the client.
4. Attention on an unfixable file (D18 = 4A): copy names the row and whose problem it is: "This file's own balance column doesn't add up: off by $12.40 at row 41 (Sep 14, UBER *TRIP)." Primary action "Export it again from your bank" opens the upload sheet for the same account; secondary "Tell us about this file" sends the run id and failing row numbers (never row contents) to the tenant's support contact, which is the founder in Milestone 1; the account stays on its last good version and its row reads "Showing data through <date>"; the audited override joins as a third action in Milestone 4. The enclave rule holds: nothing from the file leaves the deployment in the report.
5. Explain-this-number (D19 = 5A): only structured components are explainable. Every table, transaction-list and chart component carries a figure id and a trailing "Show the rows" text button (44pt). Tapping opens the figure sheet, which calls explain_number(figure_id) directly against the ledger at the figure's recorded snapshot version, with no model in the path, and renders the existing transaction-list component: the number and its label as shown in chat, the rows newest first, and a footer "computed from <account> through <date> · version <n>". Numbers inside prose are not tappable through Milestone 3; Milestone 4 may ask the model to emit a figure component beside prose totals. Chat service change: the numeric components already carry figure_id; the client adds the control.
6. "Data as of" (D20 = 6A): a fixed line rendered by the client from a new GET /snapshot endpoint (latest committed version, per-source coverage_through and state), never generated by the model. All sources agree: "Data as of Sep 28". Sources disagree: the latest date plus the limiting source, "Data as of Sep 28 · Amex through Aug 31". A blocked or disconnected source: "Data as of Sep 28 · QuickBooks disconnected since Tue". Every connect row shows its own "through <date>". The model's data context keeps the same facts so answers agree; the greeting prompt gains a rule not to restate the date. This replaces the engineering plan's "earliest coverage_through" rule for the greeting.
7. Re-upload replacement (D21 = 7A): when a re-upload would retire any rows, the run pauses in needs_confirmation with a preview: "This file covers Aug 1 to Aug 31. It adds 4 lines and replaces 92. 3 lines in your current data aren't in this file." Primary "Replace"; secondary "Keep both, don't replace" imports only the new rows (dedup guarantees no double count). An exact duplicate commits as a success: "Nothing new. All 96 lines were already in." Retirements are listed in the report and are reversible by re-uploading the earlier file. Engineering change: the coverage-window retirement runs only after this confirmation; the run state machine gains the preview payload.

Gaps the reference exposes. Revision 2 pulls all six into Milestone 1 (see Security and tenancy); listed here as the record of where they came from:
1. Verified identity: Kong validates the JWT and mints the customer id; Penny's middleware reads claims unverified, and the iOS Keychain credential (D13) has no issuer. An auth gateway or signed-token issuer is required before any real customer.
2. Guardrails: Model Armor / Bedrock Guardrails on every prompt and response. Penny has ToolCallValidator and ModelCallLimit only; nothing for prompt injection carried inside uploaded descriptors, or for leakage in model text.
3. Durable sessions: the plan never moved sessions out of process memory; two chat containers would split a conversation. Sessions belong in the ledger database.
4. Consent record: the privacy line (D6) shows a promise; nothing records that a tenant accepted terms. Add a consent table written at provisioning and on change.
5. Edge: no CDN or WAF is named in the deployment section.
6. Prompt and response retention: the audit table records events; whether prompts and responses are retained, and for how long, must be stated under the enclave rule.
